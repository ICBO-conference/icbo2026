Accepted Abstracts¶

Poster

Cell Line Ontology in 2026¶

Jie Zheng, Stella Liao, Abigail Samuelson, Yongqun He University of Michigan Medical School, Ann Arbor, MI, USA

The Cell Line Ontology (CLO), an OBO Foundry member initiated in 2010, contains more than 43,000 terms standardizing the representation of cell lines from ATCC, HyperCLDB, and other repositories. After a pause in active development, CLO development resumed in 2026 and several major issues were identified and resolved: 609 ROBOT-reported errors (duplicate labels, duplicate definitions, and multiple definitions), imported terms that had been deprecated or replaced in their source ontologies, and inconsistent application of the cell line cell design pattern. CLO was also realigned from BFO to the Core Ontology for Biology and Biomedicine (COB) to strengthen interoperability, and a Makefile-based release workflow using OntoFox and ROBOT templates was implemented. These updates support richer queries over cell lines by derived cell type, anatomical structure, source organism, disease model, and repository.

Keywords: ontology, Cell Line Ontology, cell line

Software Demo

CellCards 2.0: An Ontology-Driven Platform for Anatomically Contextualized Cell and Cell Line Knowledge Integration¶

Feng-Yu Yeh, Mary Czelusniak, Stella Liao, Jie Zheng, William D. Duncan, Alexander D. Diehl, Bruce W. Herr II, Katy Börner, Yongqun He University of Michigan; University of Florida College of Dentistry; NF/SG VAHS; University at Buffalo; Indiana University

CellCards is an ontology-driven knowledgebase for the standardized characterization of cell types. Because identical cell types can exhibit distinct molecular and functional profiles across tissues, CellCards 2.0 maps Cell Ontology (CL) identifiers to multiple UBERON anatomical contexts, enabling tissue-specific disambiguation of primary cells, and adds over 30,000 immortalized cell lines represented through the Cell Line Ontology (CLO). The platform integrates HuBMAP ASCT+B tables and Functional Tissue Unit reference models, ingests ontology-linked metadata from over 160,000 Cellosaurus records, and retrieves CL and CLO information via Ontobee SPARQL queries. The demo showcases tissue-aware cell exploration, ontology-linked biomarker integration, and semantic disambiguation workflows across multi-source biomedical datasets.

Keywords: Cell Ontology; Cell Line Ontology; semantic integration; HuBMAP; biomedical ontologies

Poster

Connecting Neural Circuit Biotypes to Pharmacogenomics: An OWL 2 DL Ontology Extension for Precision Psychiatry Decision Support¶

Lucas Wozniak, Atul Chaudhari, Chuyi Zhang, Lauren Hamilton

Depression and anxiety disorders exhibit substantial biological heterogeneity, yet no existing system provides a unified, computable representation linking neural circuit biotype classification to pharmacogenomics safety constraints for antidepressant prescribing. This work presents a confidence-qualified neural phenotype ontology joining the two domains through an OWL 2 DL knowledge architecture, a Bayesian network for biotype inference, and a pharmacogenomics clinical decision support module. The ontology aligns to BFO 2020 and bridges via SKOS to UBERON, HPO, the Cognitive Atlas, and SNOMED CT, with accompanying SSSOM mapping tables; it validates as OWL 2 DL-conformant with zero ROBOT errors. An HL7 CDS Hooks 2.0 service combines SPARQL-queried drug–gene relationships, CPIC Activity Score rules, and FHIR Genomics Reporting IG v3.0.0 genotype data to generate real-time prescribing alerts, maintaining a strict separation between ontology vocabulary, curated knowledge base, and procedural rule engine.

Enabling Cross-Domain Semantic Data Integration with an RDF Representation of RefChem¶

Qingliang Li, Sunghwan Kim, Paul Thiessen, Jian Zhang, Tiejun Cheng, Asta Gindulyte, Siqian He, Evan Bolton National Center for Biotechnology Information, National Library of Medicine, NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA

Chemical entity integration across heterogeneous biomedical resources remains a persistent challenge, since even authoritative sources disagree over whether two representations refer to the same entity — formaldehyde as a gas versus formalin as an aqueous solution, or active pharmaceutical ingredients versus their salt forms. RefChem, the PubChem Reference Collection, disambiguates and semantically organizes chemical substances using standardized structures, names, and identifiers. This work presents an RDF-based representation of RefChem that captures substance identity, structural relationships, naming types, associations, and variants, enabling formal semantic integration within PubChemRDF and across external resources such as drugs, toxicology data, biological assays, literature, and patents, and advancing the interoperability and FAIRness of chemical data.

Poster

Semantic Mapping of Psychological Assessment Questions to BERTopic-Derived Solitude-Related Concepts Using SciBERT Embeddings¶

B. Damayanthi Jesudas, Jérémy Ravenel, Maxime Jublou, Sam Smith, Feng-Yu Yeh, Jie Zheng, Yongqun He, Regina Hurley, Julie C. Bowker, Hollen N. Reischer, John Beverley, William D. Duncan University of Florida; NF/SG VAHS; University at Buffalo; University of Michigan Medical School; NaasAI, Inc.

Part of the Promoting Healthy Aging through Semantic Enrichment of Solitude Research (PHASES) project, this study extends prior BERTopic modeling of The Handbook of Solitude by semantically linking psychological assessment questions to BERTopic-derived topic representations using SciBERT embeddings. Questions drawn from the UCLA Loneliness Scale, Differential Loneliness Scale, Inventory of Interpersonal Problems, and Subjective Happiness and Loneliness Scales were encoded alongside topic summaries, and cosine similarity identified the five most semantically similar topics per question. The framework captures relationships beyond keyword overlap and surfaces candidate psychological constructs for ontology development, while demonstrating that subject matter expert review remains necessary to separate genuinely relevant concepts from semantically related but conceptually irrelevant topics.

Keywords: SciBERT; BERTopic; semantic similarity; psychological assessment; solitude; ontology development

Poster

Tracking Usage of Ontologies in the Translational Science Ecosystem¶

Sebastian Duesing, Fabrício Rodrigues, Barry Smith Department of Philosophy, University at Buffalo, Amherst, NY, USA

Translational research projects use ontologies to annotate, analyze, and harmonize diversely formatted data, and both the ontologies and the projects benefit when developers understand how their ontologies are used. At present, however, it is almost impossible for open-source ontology development teams to identify where their ontologies are being used outside their own laboratories. Ontology browsers such as Ontobee and the Ontology Lookup Service expose inter-ontology dependencies, but comparable visibility into downstream project use does not exist. The authors propose a registry of ontology usage in translational research (ROUTR) to collect and disseminate information about how clinical translational science projects use and depend on ontologies.

Keywords: ontology, ontology usage, clinical translational science, database

Using MedGen to Assign Clinical Categories to Tests and Utilize the Disease Categories as a Search Criterion for Tests in the NIH Genetic Testing Registry (GTR®)¶

Thilakam Venkatapathi, Adriana Malheiro, Megan Kane, Marilu Hoeppner, Baoshan Gu, George Zhou, Doug Hoffman, Sahithi Punyasamudram, Brandi Kattman National Center for Biotechnology Information, National Library of Medicine, NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA

MedGen integrates clinically relevant and sometimes orthogonal ontologies and vocabularies — including HPO, Mondo, OrphaNet, a UMLS subset, OMIM, PharmGKB, GeneReviews, and GARD — under one resource, mapping equivalent concepts to a single MedGen record and routing automated-pipeline discrepancies to curators for review. To help clinicians select suitable tests in the NIH Genetic Testing Registry, this work assigns broad clinical specialty labels to tests: higher-level HPO and Mondo nodes are matched to each clinical category label, descendants inherit that label, and the labels collected across a test's indicated diseases are scored by how frequently they are represented in order to prioritize display. The resulting clinical categories now power retrieval of tests by broad clinical category in GTR's advanced search.
